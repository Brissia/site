If 2024 taught me anything, it is that the startups that survive are not the most popular, or even the ones with lots of funding and unlimited resources… They are the ones that know what to measure and how to make decisions that optimize their processes so they can then automate them and, above all, optimize their acquisition channels and their use of resources to find a growth model… That way you don't have to burn thousands and thousands of dollars to learn! We enter 2025 with more uncertainty, more competition and less room for error. Improvising is no longer an option, and neither is getting lost in the sweetness of *vanity metrics*.

So, if you have a startup or lead a growing company, **what should you measure in 2025?**

And you will say… "Bri, the NSM thing again?" And yes! Or do you want to go around like a deaf person in a shootout, as my mom used to say.

## 1. The North Star Metric: one metric, one north…

It is the indicator that inspires your team to keep going, while having a direct impact on your revenue and being perfectly traceable. It gives you clarity about every action and initiative you have. It aligns the team so that every effort, resource and hour is dedicated only to putting you one step closer to your vision.

Some examples:

- **Slack**: Messages sent within an active team.
- **Spotify**: Minutes listened per user.
- **Airbnb**: Nights booked.

But my favorite is without a doubt the NSM of a hospital in the United States:

> Until every child is well.

The most common mistake is defining a metric that doesn't reflect real value. If you don't have your NSM yet, 2025 should start there. Because if you can't identify **what moves your business**, how are you going to scale it?

---

## 2. Retention Rate: easier to keep than to acquire…

Retention is the metric that separates the companies that grow from the ones playing at growing. What good are 50K registered users if 99% never activated and a similar percentage never came back? Because, at the end of the day, if your product can't get users to return, something isn't working, and it was only a “successful” marketing campaign that you surely paid, or wasted, a fortune on.

Analyze retention **by cohorts** (groups of users who arrived in the same period) and ask yourself:

- Who comes back after month 1?
- Which segments are most loyal, and why?
- What happens with the ones who leave?

Retention doesn't only tell you how strong your product is, but how scalable your acquisition will be. If retention is low, it doesn't matter how much you invest in marketing: **you are filling a leaky bucket**.

---

## 3. Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) vs Lifetime Value (LTV): the sustainability equation

**CAC** (Customer Acquisition Cost) measures how much it costs you to get a new user. **LTV** (Lifetime Value) measures how much money that user generates over their whole life as a customer.

- If your LTV doesn't exceed your CAC, **you are not building a sustainable business**.
- If the gap between the two narrows, you need to optimize retention or adjust your acquisition strategy.

A profitable 2025 demands going over this equation with a magnifying glass.

---

## 4. Growth Loops: what will make you grow organically

*Growth loops* are repeating cycles that generate growth in an organic, scalable way. Unlike one-off marketing campaigns (which depend on budget), loops are more efficient and sustainable.

Classic examples:

- **Dropbox**: If you refer a friend, you both get extra space → the friend joins, refers someone else → organic growth.
- **TikTok**: One user's content attracts new viewers → those viewers create more content → the cycle feeds itself.

Ask yourself:

- What mechanisms can you build so your product goes viral?
- How can you get users to bring in more users?

2025 will be the year to think about growth beyond ad campaigns on Meta or Google, handing them thousands of dollars (which, at least in Bolivia, we don't have lol).

---

## 5. Churn Rate: who is leaving, and why?

*Churn* measures the number of users or customers who abandon your product. It is not only important to know the number, but to understand the **why**:

- Which part of the product doesn't meet their expectations?
- What is the breaking point where they decide to leave?

Reducing *churn* should be an obsession. Because, in a competitive market, winning back the trust of a lost customer is much harder than keeping them.

---

## What not to measure

This is the year to leave **vanity metrics** behind and divorce the marketing agency that only brings you:

- Registered users who never activated.
- Likes and visits that don't convert.
- User base growth without retention.

If your metrics don't connect with the real growth of the business, you are not measuring, you are just entertaining yourself and playing CEO.

---

## In conclusion: measure less, but measure better! Do me the favor…

2025 is not going to forgive startups that don't understand **what moves their business**. Define your **North Star Metric**, optimize your retention, review your LTV/CAC and build growth *loops* that let you scale.

Measuring is not just reporting numbers; it is understanding the story the data is telling and making decisions from it. Because in a world of limited resources, **knowing what to measure is the smartest strategy you can have.**
