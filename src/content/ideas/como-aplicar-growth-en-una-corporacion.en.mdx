Remember when agile methodologies like Scrum and Lean became popular and corporations tried to fold them into the way they managed projects? In Bolivia, at least, we saw plenty of embarrassing failures along the lines of...: “We are agile” but... not that much.

Well, the same thing is happening with #Growth. Most people associate it with startups, fast experimentation and small teams iterating at the speed of light. But waway, what happens when we take this mindset into a **corporation**?

Established companies have heavy structures, hierarchies and processes that can make applying growth strategies feel like moving inside a bowl of jelly. Even so, companies that have managed to make **growth a strategic pillar** have not only stayed relevant, they have come to dominate their industries.

Growth doesn't just mean more revenue. It means **expanding strategic options**, unlocking new opportunities and creating the **flexibility to navigate increasingly uncertain markets or crisis environments**. McKinsey & Company frames it with its model of the "*Seven Degrees of Freedom for Growth"*, and here I tell you how to apply them inside an established company.

## 1️⃣ Expand with existing products

Making the most of your current customer base and selling them more is the fastest road to growth. The key is not just pushing more sales, but **understanding what moves your customers**, what they value most and how you can strengthen the relationship to maximize their *Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)*.

**Example:** Amazon with *Prime* and its ecosystem of products and benefits designed to raise each user's average order value.

## 2️⃣ Acquire new customers

You can't depend only on your current base. **Widening your market reduces risk and gives you more room to maneuver.** This is where *growth hacking* comes in, with acquisition strategies that are more efficient, less dependent on budget and more grounded in optimization.

**Example:** Uber creating referral programs that turned every user into a brand ambassador.

## 3️⃣ Develop new offerings

Innovation is not just launching new products out of inertia. It is about finding **real opportunities for expansion**, based on data and market needs. A company with robust processes but no innovation is destined to become irrelevant.

**Example:** Netflix went from being a DVD rental service to dominating streaming, and now even content production.

## 4️⃣ Reimagine how value is delivered

It is not only *what* you sell, but *how* you deliver it. Optimizing user experience and distribution models can be the differentiator that lifts your positioning.

**Example:** Tesla cutting out intermediaries and selling cars directly to the consumer. Or a case closer to home: Yape with its distinctive “yape” sound when a transaction goes through, which also plays with neuro-auditory programming.

## 5️⃣ Expand geographically

Entering new markets is not only growing, it is **diversifying risk**. Big corporations do it all the time, but not all of them understand that, to scale in new territories, the key is **local adaptation**, not copying and pasting the model. Many don't get this, and instead of focusing on enriching the *inputs* they demand *outputs*... big mistake!

**Example:** Mercado Libre understood that every country has a different culture around payments, logistics and consumer habits, so it created specific solutions for each region. But it didn't ask its country managers for results in the third month; it focused instead on giving them every resource to understand their markets.

## 6️⃣ Redefine the industry

The rules of the game are not written in stone. The companies that manage to **change the norms** of their sector are the ones that end up leading. **Why compete on someone else's terms when you can write your own rules?**

**Example:** Apple with its App Store, creating an ecosystem where developers generate the content and Apple simply takes a commission.

## 7️⃣ Compete in new spaces

Entering markets with no real competition **gives you an absolute advantage**. The key is to identify *blue oceans*, those spaces where you can operate without immediate pressure from other players.

**Example:** SpaceX doing what only governments could do before, with commercial space exploration.

## And you will say: OK Bri! But where do we start??

The mistake many big companies make is assuming that **growth only applies to startups**. But the reality is that the principles are universal:

✅ **Fast iteration** instead of waiting for "the perfect strategy".

✅ **Continuous experimentation** with real data, not with assumptions, the boss's intuition or mere whims.

✅ **Optimizing the business model** without fear of internal disruption.

Big companies shouldn't be asking themselves **"can we apply growth?"**, but **“why aren't we doing it yet?"**.

## So, since I love making your life easier.

From the other side of the world (Türkiye 🇹🇷) I bring you 6 frameworks I use when I am mentoring a corporation, to apply the fundamentals of Growth and clear the path toward a future without uncertainty:

## 1. Define what makes you unique

**Framework:** Porter's Value Chain

**Step:** Identify your strengths and differentiators.

**Result:** Focus on what sets you apart from the competition.

---

## 2. Analyze the opportunities

**Framework:** BCG Growth-Share Matrix

**Step:** Classify your products or services by their growth potential.

**Result:** Invest where it matters and remove what holds you back.

---

## 3. Align strategy across the whole organization

**Framework:** McKinsey 7S Model

**Step:** Connect your strategy, structure and systems.

**Result:** Make sure every effort supports your main goals.

---

## 4. Set a clear vision

**Framework:** SWOT Analysis

**Step:** Assess strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats.

**Result:** Build an actionable roadmap for growth.

---

## 5. Balance innovation and execution

**Framework:** McKinsey's Three Horizons

**Step:** Balance short-term wins with long-term bets.

**Result:** Stay competitive today while securing tomorrow.

---

## 6. Stay flexible

**Framework:** PEST Analysis

**Step:** Monitor political, economic, social and technological trends.

**Result:** Adapt quickly to external changes and trends.
