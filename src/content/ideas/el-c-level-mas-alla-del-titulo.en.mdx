Have you noticed that “CEO & Founder” is the most common role on LinkedIn? But what does it really mean to be a Chief Executive Officer? And likewise… CFO, CMO, COO and a very long etc.? What is behind the elegant offices on Olympus, and what does taking on one of these roles really involve? Easy as it is to crown yourself with a “Chief” for any purpose… I think it is important to talk about the real expectations we should have of these profiles inside our companies.

Here I want to draw an analogy between the C-Suite and a chessboard… Yes, even though I never learned to play because my father deliberately never taught me (but that is a story for another article), I know the rules, the game and its implications very well. So come with me and imagine a *dream team*, and the expectations I personally have of the C-Level.

♔ **CEO: The King - The visionary.**

✓ The CEO defines the strategy and secures the company's future.

✓ Represents the company before shareholders and the board.

✓ Drives innovation and opens new opportunities.

✓ Builds relationships with stakeholders.

♕ **CFO: The Queen - The financial powerhouse.**

✓ Drives strategy and financial health.

✓ Manages budgets, capital structure and financial planning.

✓ Ensures compliance with regulations.

✓ Oversees investor relations and financial reporting.

♖ **COO: The Rook - The operational backbone.**

✓ Oversees production, quality control and service delivery.

✓ Optimizes processes and policies for maximum efficiency.

✓ Makes sure teams are aligned with strategic goals.

♗ **CMO: The Bishop - The strategist.** (please, not a by-the-book marketer)

✓ Develops acquisition, retention and growth strategies.

✓ Has a unique sensitivity for new business and opportunities.

✓ Monitors market trends and adjusts tactics.

♘ **CIO: The Knight - The innovator.**

✓ Leads technology strategy and innovation.

✓ Oversees digital transformation and data security.

✓ Aligns technology with business goals.

♙ **CRO: The Pawn - The protector.**

✓ Designs and implements risk management strategies.

✓ Monitors compliance with policies and regulations.

✓ Prepares reports to keep leadership informed.

---

Each role has its own moves, its own priorities and its own strategy. The CEO wants to grow at all costs, the CFO demands financial discipline, the COO looks for operational efficiency and the CMO needs more budget to scale. So far, so good.

But what happens when these agendas collide? Strategies contradict each other. Priorities scatter. Execution wobbles.

Sound familiar?

Companies don't fail because they lack capable leaders; they fail because **they can't get aligned**. And Olympus is not just a group of pretty titles: it is a system where each role has a key function that, if it is out of sync, ends in checkmate.

## The big problem: every player pulls their own way

If the CEO pushes to grow, but the CFO says there is no money and the COO has no way to execute… **boom**, game over.

Success is not about each leader being brilliant in their role, but about how they manage to **align and complement each other to move the pieces together**. The CEO's vision needs capital, discipline, execution and innovation to work. No role can play alone.

**OK Bri, so then what? How do I align the C-Level?**

In my experience, one way to align Olympus is to involve them in defining the strategy, to create spaces that foster collective intelligence beyond ego and, above all, to make the resources, methodologies and skills available so that they fulfill their role oriented not only to results, but to a larger purpose (in corporations, perhaps the famous “vision”, and let's see if they give it a bit more meaning than being just a nice slogan…)

Strategic, analytical and, above all, critical thinking are fundamental in this team. When was the last time you heard a C-Level question their own absolute truth?? Exaaactly!! #Checkmate
