How long does it take a country to build a truly robust startup ecosystem? I am not just talking about having incubators, hubs or venture capital (which is what we all see). I am talking about the invisible part: **the institutional capacity, the regulatory maturity and the culture of innovation that allow ideas not only to be born, but to scale.**

Colombia, ranked 38th in the world, has consolidated its place as the second most relevant ecosystem in Latin America thanks to its strategic geographic position and its growing technological maturity.

Chile, for its part, has become an excellent test market: its size pushes startups to scale early, and it leads the region in FinTech regulation, as well as ranking first in InsurTech.

Both Colombia and Peru are making steady progress on regulatory frameworks for the FinTech sector. The active participation of their players in roundtables with regulators reflects a regional commitment to security, innovation and a stronger entrepreneurial ecosystem. BriGPT throws me this data:

At a lunch with the main connectors of the startup ecosystem in Bolivia (thank you [Beto Saavedra](https://www.linkedin.com/in/betosaavedra) for including me), we were asking ourselves that very question... How long will it take Bolivia to build this ecosystem? We are 5 years and more than 500 startups in. What are we missing? How are we doing? What do we want?

Bolivia is still in its **Minimum Viable Ecosystem** phase: a lot of passion, a structure taking shape, but little support. And if we think about it, developing an innovative country is a lot like developing a startup: both have to **define their North Star and find their Product Market Fit.**

## But Bri! How long does it take to find Product Market Fit?

A while ago I came across a chart showing how long it had taken the unicorns 🦄 and, as you can see, [Canva](https://www.linkedin.com/company/canva/), [Airtable](https://www.linkedin.com/company/airtable/) **and** [GitHub](https://www.linkedin.com/company/github/) (our beloved octocat) got there in one year, others in three, and some took more than five. Several never arrived.

PMF is not a milestone or a launch date, and much less a revenue figure. It is an ongoing conversation between your product, your market and reality. And as a mentor to more than 50 startups, I can tell you this clearly: **PMF is not reached with a single iteration, but with a fierce discipline of listening and adjusting, traceability and metrics.**

## OK, but how do I know I am close?

There is no magic formula, but there are tangible signals that mark the way:

- Your customers **use the product again and again. (WAU)**
- **They pay you without hesitating. (# Tickets)**
- **Retention improves** (and you no longer depend so much on paid marketing).
- **Growth becomes organic**: recommendations, mentions, community.
- **CAC &lt; LTV**: when your unit economics finally stop bleeding.

And if you work in sectors like **Agtech, HealthTech or EdTech**, that last point, organic recommendation, is literally the oxygen of the model. In markets where trust is worth more than technology, validation isn't bought: it is earned.

## So, waway...

Finding, or making, the Product Market Fit of a country, and of a startup, takes time. It takes humility to iterate, policies that back risk, and a community that understands that failing is not going backwards, but refining hypotheses.

And just as a startup needs users who believe in it, an ecosystem needs institutions that bet without fear. Because without smart regulation, talent leaves; and without a growth mindset, ideas wither before they bloom.
