I started the week looking for a designer. I ended it building a team.

I needed to renew the company's sales materials for our landing in Uruguay: one-pagers by industry, general presentations, commercial proposals and so on. Before commissioning anything, I had to understand the context: the Uruguayan customer, the momentum of each vertical, the local signals. I was getting ready to do deep research with BriGPT so I could hand a complete brief to a graphic designer who would develop these materials in time.

I posted the classic "looking for a corporate graphic designer" on LinkedIn. What I got back were social media portfolios. Templates, reels, carousels. Nothing in the institutional register I needed.

And then...

## Monday: creating a skill in Claude with the brief BriGPT wrote

I loaded the full brand guide, the use cases by industry and the research I had already done on the momentum of each vertical in Uruguay. I extrapolated to Latam while I was at it. Today, with a single instruction, I invoke the skill:

*"I have a meeting with FEMSA, the Coca-Cola bottler in Uruguay. Design a one-pager with Ciudata's CPG and consumer goods use cases for the industry."*

The result was spectacular. [You can see it here.](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1v4-T90ijEAss23XFrgXYxbkg8uVttTlO/view?usp=sharing) Obviously, I ran out of credits instantly and had to subscribe. Cheap for what I was getting in return.

## Tuesday: What if I build my own SDR?

I decided to design an SDR for my acquisition channel strategy. Anyone who has worked with lead generation companies knows it: onboarding is like giving birth. They never quite understand your solution, few of them respect your ICP, and while they do get meetings with middle or senior management, it is not always the right buyer persona. After years with that format, I already have my BriGPT trained to produce hyper-detailed documentation that cuts friction to a minimum.

I built the skill with that same methodology. This is how the flow turned out:

**1. Context.** What the company does, what the ICP is, who the buyer persona is, where we operate, use cases, current customers and the full pipeline with the detail of each stage. ([I have an article on how to migrate GPT's memory to Claude, in case you need it; the process is worth it.](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/brissiabenavente_c%C3%B3mo-migrar-de-chat-gpt-a-claude-notion-activity-7442947360412008449-Fjg4))

**2. Business researcher.** An agent instructed to track a curated list of sources (blogs, portals, digital newspapers, business magazines) in search of specific signals: plans to expand, territorial growth, investment in technology. All filtered by our ICP, with a set of keywords built beforehand in GPT.

**3. Mapping and integration with Apollo.** Once the agent delivers the weekly report and an opportunity appears, it maps the potential buyer personas and, through the integration with Apollo.io, gets the verified contact.

**4. Style.** This is what makes the difference. I already have another skill trained on my personal voice, the one I use for my art criticism articles and for what I publish on LinkedIn. I built a sub-skill with that style so the emails don't feel like the million templated InMails we all get every day. Claude writes hyper-personalized emails for each buyer persona and leaves them in my outbox to review and adjust if needed.

**5. Send timing.** Once I give the OK, Claude schedules the send for the best moment for the email to be read and answered.

**6. Repetition.** The cycle repeats every week. Four countries. More than three hundred companies tracked. More than a hundred sources monitored.

## The thought I am left with.

I replaced two people with two AIs. Not because people are surplus, but because those two functions, commercial research and outbound prospecting, demanded a level of understanding of my solution, my ICP and my voice that no external provider gave me without weeks of onboarding and, at best, acceptable middling results.

The AIs don't understand me better because they are intelligent. They understand me better because I trained them myself, with a precision I could never use to train a freelancer on a kickoff call. The difference is not in the tool; it is in the discipline of documenting your own judgment until it becomes an executable instruction.

Faster, more coherent, more aligned. And me, free to do what no AI is going to do for me: read the business in a way I can translate into technology, make the strategic decision, hold the relationship.

Today, Monday, the emails went out in the morning. In the next installment I will tell you how the results are going.
