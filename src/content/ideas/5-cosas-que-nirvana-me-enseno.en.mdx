## Why settle for “mediocre" when “wonderful" exists?

You know that moment when there is food in the bowl but an empty spot in the middle? (If you have a cat, you will understand me.) Nirvana was not one to accept that kind of situation. If something wasn't up to her level, she simply looked at me, with those eyes that seemed to say: "You know this is not acceptable." There was no negotiation, there were no doubts. She expected the best, because she was aware of her standards, and her expectations were completely realistic...

And I realize this post is not the typical professional or commercial content. These are 5 lessons that apply to any part of life... Being aware of our standards, and making sure our expectations are consistent with them.

## The right answer doesn't matter...

What really interests me is the iterative process of curiosity. And yes, they say curiosity killed the cat, but Nirvana lived 21 years sticking her little nose into everything! And now I realize I am happy experimenting. I am not the least bit interested in finding answers, but in trying things, observing and learning from each experience... and staying in that dynamic.

And when the learning happens, it is magic!

## Not losing the capacity to be amazed by simple things

Nirvana could spend hours in the sun, in silence, contemplating. She could stay still in front of a window, watching how the wind moved the leaves, *"the sweetness of doing nothing”.* Those who know me know that if they had to define me in one word it would be “lazy” haha, but the truth is I have learned to enjoy my mere existence enormously, with no stimulation and without the slightest guilt for doing nothing at all, like her...

And watching how some people are caught up in hyper-productivity and routine: getting up at 5 am to exercise, meditate, read a book a week, go to work, be the best “shark mind” (who knows what for, somebody just told them to), wait for the weekend to spend a fortune on partying, buy things you don't need, or kids, wait for the end of the year to spend a fortune on vacation, and repeat... (As if the movie Click hadn't taught them anything hahaha.) So I come out as “lazy” to some and, in the best of cases, “different”. In a world where everything is urgent, where we are always thinking about what comes next, she reminded me that the present is the only thing we really have. And there is nothing more valuable than learning to be in it.

## We are afraid, but we do it even with fear.

- Nirvana got lost twice, 4 days each time, and came back as if she had been to the spa.
- She lived in 3 different cities.
- We moved more than 10 times in 21 years.
- Since I am a bit of a *pataeperro*, always on the move, she had to stay with many carers for long periods of time.
- When she came home we were not at all a middle-class family, quite the opposite... so she got to know the concept of “lean years".

And yes! I am sure the little one was scared to death at times, like me, like anyone... but what an ability to handle change, uncertainty and fear... And I am not talking about #stoicism, a school of thought I have serious criticisms of (and I will surely take that on in another article). I am talking about developing the skills to recognize that we are sc4red sh\*tless and doing it anyway, only to discover that nothing is thaaat serious at the end of the day. That taught me to live without fear. What a lesson, living without fear. I recommend it!

## Like in poker!... You leave the table while you are winning.

There is a powerful lesson in the art of leaving in time: knowing when to stop is not giving up, it is strategic intelligence. I use poker as an analogy, although I am no fan of the game, but I think it makes the point... Real success is not in betting everything until the end, but in recognizing when the moment is perfect to walk away with the win. Although I know a player will tell me: "Bri, success is *bluffing* until you take it all", and that is where I disagree...

For me, Nirvana was never about moving forward without pause. And she taught me that withdrawing, even from life, is also about preserving what you have achieved, avoiding suffering, caring for others and, in the end, the art of letting go.

The challenge is to beat the ego, the one that insists on looking for more, on risking what isn't necessary for a little more validation or recognition. Accepting that "more" doesn't always mean "better" is a sign of wisdom that only 21 years could hold...

And yes, I am writing this with tears in my eyes and my heart in pieces. But above all with the hope that something of what Nirvana taught me makes sense to some of you.
