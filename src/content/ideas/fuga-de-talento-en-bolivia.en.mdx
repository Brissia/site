In recent years, brain drain in Latin America has stopped being a trend and become a structural crisis.

According to reports from the regional tech ecosystem, more than **60% of digital professionals in LATAM work, or want to work, for foreign companies**, driven mainly by better pay, flexibility and opportunities to grow. Bolivia is no exception: highly qualified talent no longer competes in local markets, but in a globalized market where the “employer” is any company in the world.

Add to this a new generation of professionals, late millennials and Gen Z, who are rewriting the rules of the game. They no longer put stability above purpose, or hierarchy above autonomy. They look for:

- Real flexibility (not symbolic)
- Accelerated growth
- A coherent organizational culture (not one that only exists in speeches)
- Whole wellbeing (mental, physical and financial)

The problem: many Bolivian companies still operate with models designed for another era. Ñaup'a tiempo, the old days, as my grandma would say...

---

### The pay gap: the elephant in the room

Let's take a concrete case.

According to regional salary guides like the one from Interfell (here is the [link](https://www.interfell.com/guia-salarial-latinoamerica) so you can check it), a **semi-senior** professional in technology (for example in cybersecurity, data or development) can earn between:

- **$2,500 and $4,500 USD a month working remotely for international companies**

Now let's compare:

- A **bank CTO in Bolivia** has a salary that ranges between **30,000 and 45,000 Bs**, roughly equivalent to **$3,200 USD a month**

This reveals a critical distortion:

👉 A global semi-senior profile can earn the same as, or more than, a local C-level.

It is not only about salary. It is about the **structural competitiveness of the Bolivian job market**.

## OK Bri! So what can change?

Here are 3 global trends

### 1. EX as an engine of business growth (not an HR initiative)

Leading organizations are no longer treating employee experience as a “nice to have” and are turning it into a direct driver of results.

The Summit stresses that EX is increasingly connected to:

- Organizational performance
- Brand value
- Sustainable growth

It is no longer about engagement, but about **business impact**.

---

### 2. Bringing technology, data and AI into talent management

Employee experience is becoming deeply data-driven.

The event highlights the growing use of:

- People analytics
- Artificial intelligence
- Predictive retention models

This makes it possible to move from intuitive decisions to intelligent systems that anticipate talent loss and optimize the experience in real time.

---

### 3. An adaptive organizational culture for hybrid, changing environments

Today's context, with hybrid work, shifting expectations and digital transformation, forces companies to redesign their culture.

The most advanced ones are:

- Rethinking leadership (more human, less hierarchical)
- Making wellbeing a strategic pillar
- Designing resilient, flexible cultures

Culture stops being a statement and becomes a living system that sustains the employee experience.
