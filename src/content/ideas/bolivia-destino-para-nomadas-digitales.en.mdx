For 8 years now I have been working remotely and traveling the world. Yes, I am living my dream of being a #DigitalNomad. And while looking for my next destination these days, I came across a very interesting fact: Portugal has established itself as the favorite for remote professionals over the last 3 years, with more than 17 thousand nomads holding its special D8 visa… and I wondered, could Bolivia have enormous potential to become an attractive destination in the digital world? What are we missing?

Costa Rica, for example, offers solid technology infrastructure, reliable internet access and a growing community of remote professionals. Places like the Guanacaste region and the town of Santa Teresa have become popular destinations for digital nomads, because they offer a unique mix of beaches, nature and a vibrant community. The Costa Rican government has also launched initiatives to attract remote professionals and entrepreneurs, such as the “Rentista Digital” program, which gives tax benefits to those who choose to settle and work in Costa Rica, generating millions of dollars a year in income for the country. In the same way, destinations in Southeast Asia, Central America and other points on the globe are betting on this new economy.

So, could Bolivia, with one foot inside the most serious economic crisis of the last 20 years, become a destination for digital nomads?

Here are three fundamental points the Bolivian government should consider to promote the country as a destination for this segment, which produces more than 2.4 trillion dollars according to a report by [**Upwork**](https://www.linkedin.com/company/upwork/).

**Technology infrastructure:** The government must invest in better technology infrastructure, ensuring a fast and reliable internet connection across the country. This is essential for digital nomads to do their work efficiently and without interruptions. So that what happened to #Speed doesn't happen to them.

**Legal and tax support:** It is crucial to set favorable policies and regulations for digital nomads, such as tax benefits and special visas for remote professionals. This will encourage foreign talent to come and create the right environment to settle and work in Bolivia.

**Promoting natural and cultural attractions:** Bolivia has cultural wealth and stunning landscapes that can attract digital nomads. The government should promote and highlight them, showing the diversity of experiences remote professionals can enjoy while working in the country.

Bolivia has the potential to become an attractive destination for digital nomads. In fact, initiatives are already emerging that aim to position the region as a strategic destination for them. **Projects like** [**RVnB**](https://www.linkedin.com/company/rvnb-bo/) **Bolivia** are leading the market. Can you imagine landing at El Alto airport and picking up your #RV, fully equipped with satellite internet, water, electricity, air conditioning and a full week of autonomy? Ready to head into Bolivia's most incredible landscapes, have breakfast with fabulous views and work from the most heavenly places, like the Salar de Uyuni, Sajama Park, a vineyard in Tarija or the middle of the Chiquitanía.

This is not just a dream, it is already a reality! So I invite you to the upcoming open-door events; the next one will be in Cochabamba.
